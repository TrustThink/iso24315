# METR Engineering Information

This section provides additional information and guidance to assist in the understanding the technical details and foundations of METR, as defined by the ISO 24315 series. Where earlier pages explain *what* METR is and *how* different organizations should approach deployment, the pages here document *how the standards were developed*, *what terms mean*, *how requirements trace to stakeholder input*, and *detailed implementation guidance*.

The intended readers are standards participants, system architects, product engineers, and others who need the technical lineage behind the ISO 24315 series — not only a high-level overview.

## What You Will Find Here

### Development process

METR was developed using systems engineering methods grounded in stakeholder engagement. The [development process](development.md) summarizes that approach, the resulting parts of the ISO 24315 series, and planned future work. The [workshops](workshops.md) page records the late-2021 public sessions that shaped early user needs and architecture choices.

### Vocabulary

Consistent terminology is essential across independently owned METR components. The [vocabulary](documentation/Vocab.md) material aligns with ISO/TS 24315-1 and supports precise reading of the operational concept, requirements, and implementation guides.

### Traceability

METR materials are maintained so that workshop inputs, operational concepts, and requirements remain linked. The [documentation index](documentation/index.md) introduces the published and draft artefacts:

- [Operational Concept (ConOps)](documentation/ConOps.md)
- [System of Systems Requirements (SoSR)](documentation/SoSR.md)
- [System-level Requirements (SysR)](documentation/SysR.md)
- [METR Data Requirements (MDR)](documentation/MDR.md)
- [Workshop summary points](documentation/SP.md) and [notes](documentation/N.md)

Use these pages when you need to see why a requirement exists, what stakeholder concern it addresses, or how architecture choices connect back to the ConOps.

### Implementation guides

Engineering information also includes deployment-oriented guidance for regional frameworks and component systems. Start with the [guide structure](ig/guides.md), then follow the guides for regional policy, rule makers, and the regulation, distribution, consumer, and discrepancy-handling systems.

Normative requirements remain in the ISO 24315 series (and regional refinements). This website makes selected engineering artefacts and guidance easier to navigate and to better explain the practical intentions behind specific requirement; it does not replace the published standards.
