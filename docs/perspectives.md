# Perspectives and Guidance

This portion of the METR website provide guidance to various stakeholders that share different perspectives of the METR system.

- **[Regional Body Perspective](regional-body-perspective.md)**: In the METR context, a region is defined as a set of jurisdictional areas where users would expect to be able to cross jurisdictional boundaries while maintaining seamless interoperable operation. This section considers the perspective of the entities that establish regional policies and guidelines to ensure interoperability and consistency across the region. It also explains how the international METR standards need to be refined into regional standards, policies, and guidelines and then further refined to identify local details (e.g., identifying responsible agencies for each component system).
- **[Jurisdictional Management Perspective](jurisdictional-management-perspective.md)**: Each jurisdiction will need to consider how its regulations will integrate into a METR deployment. Smaller jurisdictions will likely rely on a larger jurisdiction to manage the regulation system while providing them with access credentials to enter their regulations. Larger jurisdictions will need to consider how to build, operate, and maintain a regulation system for their jurisdiction - and likely for smaller jurisdictions that are contained within their area or located nearby. This section explains the benefits of METR for a jurisdictional management entity, how a jurisdictional management relates to a regulation system, and explains the complexities that need to be considered when establishing a regulation system for a regulations system. This section also explains the responsibilities of jurisdictional management in the context of METR.
- **[Rule Maker Perspective](rule-maker-perspective.md)**: Rule makers are responsible for defining the regulations that will be translated and stored in the METR system. Rule makers include legislative bodies, regulatory bodies (such as road authorities), and other entities that are responsible for defining the regulations (e.g., road operators and emergency services). This section explains the benefits of METR for rule makers, how a rule maker relates to a regulation system, and explains that different types of rule makers that are likely to exist within a jurisdiction. This section also explains the responsibilities of rule makers in the context of METR and provides guidance on how to tackle the challenge of migrating a massive number of legal regulations into an electronic environment in a manner that allows for benefits to be realized in the interim before all regulations are migrated to the METR system.
- **[Road Operator Perspective](road-operator-perspective.md)**: In addition to being a rule maker, a road operator is responsible for operating roadside devices and central system equipment to manage the roadside devices; it might also act as the jurisdictional manager and host the regulation system.  This section explains the benefits of METR for a road operator, the responsibilities of a road operator, and explains the complexities that need to be considered.
- **[System Management Perspective](system-management-perspective.md)**: Every system within the METR system of systems will be hosted by some entity that plans, develops, deploys, operates, and maintains the system. Many of these responsibilities are common across all of the component systems; this section explains the responsibilities of a system manager, and explains the complexities that need to be considered.
- **[Consumer Perspective](consumer-perspective.md)**: Guidance for entities that build or operate METR consumer systems in vehicles, apps, and related devices.
